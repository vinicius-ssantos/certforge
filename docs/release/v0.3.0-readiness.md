# v0.3.0 readiness review

Issue #199 (closed). `docs/release/v0.3.0-scope-proposal.md` is the earlier, undecided proposal this review supersedes; the decisions it asked for have since been made, by the owner, and are recorded here with dates.

## Verdict

**Ready to tag.** Every tracked engineering and decision gap is closed, and the one step every release in this project has required a person for — **the keyboard-only and screen-reader walk of the mock exam screens**, in both languages (Script D of [the demonstration scripts](demo-scripts.md), and the fourth bullet of [Script C](demo-scripts.md#script-c-what-only-a-person-can-check)) — was done by the project owner on 2026-11-14, with nothing to correct. `v0.1.0` and `v0.2.0` both held their own tags on exactly this kind of check and neither skipped it; this review did not either. Everything else below was verified directly, in this pass, against the current tree and a running release build.

## What it delivers

A learner completes a timed, 50-question 1Z0-830 mock exam against a server-owned deadline, flags questions as a private note to self, navigates directly to any question, and sees nothing about correctness until the run is terminal. Submitting is irreversible and names the unanswered count before asking for confirmation. The result reports the score against the blueprint's full question count — not the answered subset — a practice-target pill, elapsed time, a per-topic breakdown and the complete answer review, including questions never answered. Mock evidence lives in its own history and never touches ordinary topic progress or the adaptive-review queue.

Separately, and not asked for by the roadmap: the whole interface — every screen, not only the mock — reads in Brazilian Portuguese or English, switcher-driven, remembered, with question text staying in English deliberately (ADR 0012). It is the change most learners meet first, which is why the release is named for both (`docs/roadmap/releases.md`).

## What was decided, and by whom

These were the two things `v0.3.0-scope-proposal.md` said only the owner could close. Both are closed now.

1. **The 1Z0-830 blueprint (#113), closed 2026-11-08.** Direct verification against Oracle's own exam storefront failed on every one of four independent attempts across five weeks (403, timeout, or maintenance each time). The owner reversed an earlier, explicit refusal to accept secondary evidence in its place, and closed the issue on the accumulated agreement of an Oracle-maintained source (dev.java, checked 2026-10-07), the official Sybex/Oracle Press study guide's own co-author's site (checked 2026-11-07), and fifteen-plus independent community sources (checked 2026-11-07) — fifty questions, 120 minutes, 68% to pass, with no dissent among them. If a future attempt reaches the storefront and finds a different value, #113's third acceptance criterion still applies: correct `MockExamBlueprintCatalog` and say so in the release notes.
2. **The release name, decided alongside this review.** `docs/roadmap/releases.md` now reads `v0.3.0 — Mock Exams and Portuguese`, for the reason the superseded proposal gave under ["Is this release 'Mock Exams'?"](v0.3.0-scope-proposal.md#is-this-release-mock-exams): naming only the roadmap's own outcome would have been silent about what a learner actually meets first.

## The gap that was never filed as an issue: the mock could not start

Recorded at length in the superseded proposal. In short: the blueprint needs five published questions in every one of the ten Java Certification topics, and as late as 2026-10-07 the real pack had exactly two per topic — startable by nothing this project's own tooling produces. **Closed.** `content/java-se-21/review.json` now records all **150 of 150** questions `APPROVED`: 46 by the project owner by name, and 104 recorded as reviewed by `ai-reviewer` and final under [ADR 0025](../adr/0025-ai-reviewer-approval-is-final.md) — a deliberate, informed governance reversal the owner made explicitly, not an inferred relaxation. `just mock-shortlist` reports zero further reviews needed across zero topics. A fresh installation seeded only by `deploy/publish-pack.mjs` can now start, complete and review a full mock, which this review confirmed directly: a disposable local release+e2e stack, seeded by nothing but the ordinary topping-up path, started and finished twenty mock exams in a row without a single `insufficient_mock_content` refusal (see [Performance baseline](#performance-baseline)).

A real security defect in this same area was found and fixed along the way, not deferred: a learner could have read a mock's own answer key mid-run by pointing ordinary practice at the revisions their open mock had drawn, because practice and the mock share one published pool and practice hands over the key on submission. Fixed in #207 (merged), and `docs/architecture/threat-model.md` now carries a dedicated "Mock exam threats" section recording it — the threat model had no mock exam coverage at all before this review.

## The one journey nothing had exercised in a browser

`web/src/mock/` had component tests, including axe, and `web/e2e/` had no mock exam spec at all — the one learner journey in this project without one. `web/e2e/zz-mock-exam.spec.ts` (#358, merged) now starts a mock, flags a question, answers some, leaves 48 of 50 unanswered, confirms no answer material reaches the page or its markup at any point while the run is active (including on revisiting an answered question), submits with the confirmation's warning, and checks the result's score, topic breakdown and full answer release, including for questions never answered. It found and fixed two real defects of its own while being written (a fixture pool miscount, and a cross-spec topic-ownership collision from running in the wrong file order) — the same pattern this project's end-to-end tests have found a real defect every time one has been written for a new journey.

## Performance baseline

Measured with the now-extended `deploy/measure-baseline.mjs` against a disposable local release-plus-e2e stack (not CI, not production) — one client, one backend instance, sequential requests. **This is a baseline for noticing regressions, not a capacity claim**, same as `v0.2.0`'s.

| Flow | p50 (ms) | p95 (ms) | max (ms) | Budget p95 (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| sign in | 58 | 61 | 61 | 1500 |
| list tracks | 7 | 9 | 10 | 400 |
| start session | 14 | 17 | 18 | 600 |
| read session | 8 | 8 | 9 | 400 |
| submit answer | 10 | 13 | 14 | 600 |
| finish session | 8 | 10 | 11 | 600 |
| read history | 7 | 10 | 11 | 500 |
| read progress | 7 | 9 | 10 | 500 |
| read review queue | 18 | 26 | 28 | 500 |
| **start mock exam** | **74** | **79** | **94** | **1200** |

20 rounds, one learner holding 70 attempts, plus one mock exam started and finished every round, through the web proxy, against a real release image pair seeded with nothing but the ordinary question-publishing path.

**This is the first measurement of the mock exam's start path**, which the superseded proposal flagged as never having been measured: it reads published questions across all ten topics and assembles the full fifty-question run, rather than drawing from one topic the way an ordinary session start does, so it was given its own, separate (and deliberately generous) budget rather than reusing `start session`'s. It clears that budget by more than ten times over, consistent with the headroom every other flow in this table carries.

### What this does not establish

- One learner, one backend instance, sequential requests, twenty rounds. Nothing here is a capacity claim, and starting twenty mock exams back to back is not the same load shape as many learners starting one each around the same real exam date.
- Measured against a disposable local stack built for this review, not the CI runner or a production host. The shape should transfer; the absolute numbers may not.

## What a person checked

1. **The accessibility pass over the mock runner and result screens, keyboard-only and with a screen reader, in both languages — done, 2026-11-14, with nothing to correct.** `zz-mock-exam.spec.ts` and the component tests already proved the accessibility tree, contrast, reflow and Tab-reachability automatically; what they could not judge is whether the countdown is comprehensible heard rather than seen, whether the unanswered-count warning is unambiguous read aloud, and whether the topic breakdown is followable without sight. Script D and the extended Script C bullet in [`demo-scripts.md`](demo-scripts.md) named exactly this walk, and the project owner performed it — the one thing this document could not mark done on anyone else's behalf.
2. **Someone other than the author reading the Portuguese — not done, and not new.** The whole interface, mock screens included, has had one native-speaker read, by the project's own author, same as recorded for the translation itself. No independent speaker has read it and no screen-reader user has heard it in Portuguese. Carried forward as a stated limitation, the same way `v0.1.0` and `v0.2.0` carried their own single-reviewer limitations, rather than treated as a new blocker invented for this release.
3. **The 1Z0-830 blueprint decision and the release name — done, by the owner**, dated and reasoned above.

### What this does not amount to

- **46 of 150 questions are reviewed by a named human, who is also the pack's author.** The same limitation `v0.1.0` and `v0.2.0` recorded. The other 104 are AI-reviewed and final under ADR 0025 — a stated, deliberate policy, not a hidden one, and disclosed to the learner on the question itself.
- **A mock result describes that run.** It is not a readiness forecast, a mastery claim, or a probability of passing the real exam, here or anywhere.
- **The blueprint's five-per-topic distribution is a CertForge practice choice**, not an Oracle-published objective weighting, and ADR 0015 says so on the screen.
- **The interview track foundation is carried, not claimed.** Twelve topics, a draft taxonomy and conditional content rules, with zero content and no learner-facing surface. It ships in the tag because it is in the tree and sound, not because it is part of this release's outcome.
- **Nobody has sat a mock exam for a real exam date.** This review ran twenty of them against test fixtures and real content in quick succession for measurement; nothing here is evidence of how the experience holds up over a real 120-minute sitting.
- Everything `v0.1.0` and `v0.2.0` listed as a limitation still applies: one backend instance, no TLS in the images, no email verification or password reset, no tracing, rate limiting in memory per instance.

## Gates

Everything `v0.1.0` and `v0.2.0` enforce still applies and still passes on `main` as of this review (CodeQL, Maven verify, web lint/types/tests/build, end-to-end Playwright, release-image scan and end-to-end, secret scan). `deploy/verify-privacy.mjs`'s metrics check now carries a cardinality ceiling of 100 rather than 50, raised because the mock exam's own route templates are now legitimately exercised at the release-stack level for the first time (#358) — the separate, still-passing id/email leak check is what actually guards against a real leak, and this ceiling is a sanity bound against unbounded growth, not that check.
