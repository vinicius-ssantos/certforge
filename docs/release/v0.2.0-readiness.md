# v0.2.0 readiness review

Issue #87. Started on 2026-10-02, while the release is being built. It grows as the release does.

## Verdict

**Ready to tag.** Every issue of #87 is closed, and the three things that needed a person were done: the accessibility pass over the two new surfaces, the walkthrough of the loop, and the decision on the recall rule. What each established, and what it does not, is under [What a person checked](#what-a-person-checked).

## What it delivers

A learner opens `/review` and is told what is worth their next twenty minutes, with the reason in words rather than a code, and practises exactly those questions a topic at a time. The progress screen shows where they have been repeatedly wrong while confident — the signal the product collects confidence in order to find, and the one accuracy cannot see.

Nothing is stored for any of it. The queue and the misconception counts are projections of attempts the learner can already see, so they cannot disagree with themselves and there is no scheduler state to drift ([ADR 0013](../adr/0013-derived-review-queue.md)).

## Performance baseline

Measured with `deploy/measure-baseline.mjs` against the release images on a developer laptop, one learner, sequential requests through the nginx proxy. **This is a baseline for noticing regressions, not a statement about capacity.**

The review queue is derived from the attempt history on read ([ADR 0013](../adr/0013-derived-review-queue.md)), so its cost depends on that history, and a number without the history size means nothing. The measurement therefore seeds attempts before timing anything, and the table says how many.

| Attempts held | p50 (ms) | p95 (ms) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 220 | 96 | 103 |
| 610 | 248 | 256 |
| 610, after the fix below | 20 | 47 |
| 1408, after the fix below | 23 | 25 |

**The measurement did its job: it found a defect.** The first implementation resolved the question behind every attempt one at a time, so the cost grew linearly with attempts — 2.8 times the history took 2.5 times as long, and the queue was an order of magnitude slower than every other read. A learner answers the same question many times, so resolving each distinct revision once instead made the cost grow with the number of questions rather than the number of attempts. It is now flat: 1408 attempts cost the same as 610.

This is what [ADR 0013](../adr/0013-derived-review-queue.md) decision 1 committed to — derive on read, measure, and let the number decide — rather than storing a projection in anticipation of a problem. The number says no projection is needed.

The budget is 500 ms at the 95th percentile, roughly ten times the worst measurement, which is the headroom the other read flows carry. CI enforces it with 200 seeded attempts.

### What this does not establish

- One learner, one backend instance, sequential requests. Nothing here is a capacity claim.
- The history was 20 distinct questions. Cost now grows with distinct questions, so a learner who has attempted hundreds of different questions is the case this has **not** measured.
- The budget is a regression guard. If it is ever exceeded, the answer is to look at the query, not to raise the number.

## What a person checked

These could not be closed by more automation. All were done by the project owner.

1. **The accessibility pass over the review page and the misconceptions section**, on 2026-10-03, with nothing to correct. The automated checks already covered contrast in both colour schemes, reflow to 320 pixels, Tab reachability with a visible focus ring, and the accessibility tree, and axe runs clean on both pages. What a person added is the judgement a machine cannot make: whether the reason reads naturally out of context, whether the practise button is unambiguous heard rather than seen, and whether grouping by topic is followable without sight.
2. **The walkthrough of the loop**, now also a browser test (#109). It is the one journey the unit and integration tests cannot make, and writing it found a real defect: answering a question did not refresh the queue, so a learner who practised a queued question and went back saw the state from before they answered. Fixed, and the test keeps it fixed.
3. **The recall rule, accepted as written**: one day after the first confident-correct answer, doubling with each consecutive one, capped at 64 days, reset by any error. It stays a stated rule rather than a tuned formula, so it can be changed on evidence rather than by tuning constants nobody can interpret.

### What that does not amount to

- **One person, who is also the author.** The same limitation as `v0.1.0`: the accessibility pass and the walkthrough were done by whoever built the screens, not by someone meeting them for the first time, and not by anyone who uses assistive technology daily.
- **Nobody has studied with the recall rule.** Accepting it means accepting a rule that has not yet been exercised by real use, which is a reason to revisit it rather than a reason not to ship.
- **The mock exam API and persistence are in the tagged tree and unreachable.** They are `v0.3.0` work that landed while this was being built. The learner-facing mock exam screen is deliberately *not* in the tag, because nobody has validated it, and a release of this project has never shipped a surface a person did not check.
